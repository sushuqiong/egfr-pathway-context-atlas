# A curated cross-disease transcriptomic resource with growth-factor and tissue-composition annotations

**Abstract**
Growth-factor pathway modules are scored routinely in bulk tissue transcriptomes, yet scores from different studies are rarely comparable and each user repeats the same curation work. This resource reorganises public human tissue transcriptomes into a comparable, annotation-rich form. It contains 26 case-control expression cohorts across ten contexts (2711 assay records from 27 curated series of which 26 form the case-control resource, 1086 patients with available identifiers across those 26 cohorts, 12 platform annotations), sample-level annotation with tissue type, case-control role, paired relations and exclusion reasons, harmonised gene mapping with a transformation log, seventeen growth-factor module definitions with per-platform gene coverage, per-sample module scores, expression-derived composition scores with four compartments, donor-level and cell-level summaries from four single-cell datasets, a complete estimates layer that retains non-significant, not-estimable and not-testable results, validation records that separate exact reproduction from rank agreement, runnable reuse examples and the software environment. Scores are cohort-internal; cross-cohort comparison is supported through effect sizes, and the comparability of those effects is quantified rather than assumed. The resource is deposited with a persistent identifier.

**Background & Summary**
Module scores for RTK and growth-factor signalling are treated as if they measured a comparable quantity across studies, yet they do not: platforms differ in gene coverage, cohorts differ in tissue composition and design, and processed matrices differ in transformation history. A user who wants to test whether a module differs between tumour and normal tissue across diseases must first locate comparable series, resolve sample identity and tissue type, map probes to gene symbols, decide how to handle composition, and then repeat the exercise for the next cohort.

This resource performs that curation once, documents it, and quantifies how far it can be trusted. It provides (i) a cohort registry with design, platform, counts, origin publications and provenance; (ii) sample-level annotation including tissue type, case-control role, pairing and exclusion reasons; (iii) gene-mapping and transformation provenance for every cohort together with a runnable script that rebuilds one series from its public accession to module scores; (iv) module definitions with per-platform coverage and an explicit statement that a module measured with 33% of its members is not the same quantity as the full module; (v) per-sample module scores with a stated construction rule for each layer; (vi) expression-derived composition scores keyed by sample identifier, with a signature-overlap audit that bounds how far they can be treated as independent covariates; (vii) donor-level summaries from single-cell datasets with raw and curated labels kept separate; (viii) a complete estimates layer in which every cohort-by-module pair is accounted for, including the pairs that are not estimable and why; and (ix) validation records that state what was reproduced exactly, what was reproduced in rank order only, and what remains a sensitivity analysis.

**Methods**
*Cohort assembly and curation flow.* Public series were sought for ten contexts (lung adenocarcinoma, colorectal, gastric, pancreatic, hepatocellular and oesophageal squamous cancer; inflammatory bowel disease; chronic obstructive pulmonary disease; non-alcoholic fatty liver disease; asthma). A series entered the case-control resource only if case and control samples were measured on the same platform. Of the 27 series with processed matrices in this workspace, 26 qualified as case-control cohorts and one (GSE16879, 43 samples, Responder versus NonResponder) was excluded because it is a treatment-response design; a further colorectal series (GSE39582) was analysed only as an external validation set from its public accession. The full screening and exclusion counts are shipped as 08_qc/curation_flow.csv. Paired designs are derived from sample metadata, not assumed: a cohort is analysed as paired when at least four patients contribute both a case and a control sample (15 cohorts, and 689 patients in total); in paired cohorts only complete pairs enter the paired effect estimate, and the remaining samples are not pooled into it. 9 cohorts provide no patient identifiers, so pairing cannot be established and the analysis is unpaired by necessity, which is recorded per cohort.
*Expression processing.* Within each cohort, probes were mapped to gene symbols, multiple probes per gene were collapsed by the mean, unmappable probes were dropped and legacy symbols were updated where unambiguous. Matrices were log2-transformed where the input distribution indicated raw intensities; the observed distribution, the decision and the outcome are recorded per cohort in the transformation log. No cross-cohort merging or batch correction was performed.
*Module definitions, coverage and score construction.* Seventeen modules were defined a priori and frozen with a version identifier (module_version = v12.0-frozen-2026-09). Full membership is shipped together with per-platform coverage; coverage is incomplete in part of the cohorts, with the largest gaps in RET_ALK_NTRK (minimum coverage 0.33); RAS_MAPK (minimum coverage 0.58); FGFR_AXIS (minimum coverage 0.62). Score construction is layer-specific and stated explicitly: in the TCGA layer a module score is the mean over module members of the per-gene z-score across patients (gene values log2(x+1), z-scored within the context, averaged per patient across genes, then averaged across members, requiring at least two members); in the bulk cohorts it is a GSVA value z-scored within the cohort; in the single-cell layer it is the mean of log-normalised member-gene expression per cell, averaged to donor means before testing. These three quantities are not interchangeable, and the recommended layer for each use case is listed in Supplementary Table S8.
*Effects and pooling.* For each cohort and module the effect is the standardized difference between case and control samples computed on the shared root-mean-square denominator sqrt((sd_case^2+sd_control^2)/2): SMDH for unpaired cohorts, SMCRPH with the cohort's empirical within-pair correlation for paired cohorts. Every cohort-by-module pair is present in the shipped estimates table: 434 pairs are estimated and 8 are reported as not estimable with a reason (not estimable: only 2 of 3 module members present in this cohort; not estimable: only 2 of 6 module members present in this cohort). Across cohorts, effects are pooled per context and module with REML and the Hartung-Knapp adjustment; DerSimonian-Laird, ad hoc Knapp-Hartung, unpaired-only and fixed-correlation (rho = 0.5 and 0.7) analyses are shipped as sensitivity analyses, because paired cohorts carry smaller sampling variances and therefore more weight.
*Tissue composition.* Composition was estimated with xCell v1.1.0, which returns expression-derived enrichment scores rather than cell proportions; four aggregated compartments (epithelial, fibroblast, endothelial, immune) are shipped keyed by sample identifier, with the join audit. Two limitations are quantified rather than asserted away: the compartment scores and the module scores are computed from the same expression matrix, and every module shares genes with the xCell signature panel (17 of 17 modules share more than 20% of their members; maximum 100%), so the base-versus-adjusted comparison is a sensitivity analysis and not an attempt to isolate an independent composition effect; and because disease status and composition are themselves correlated (overall model R-squared median 0.33, maximum 0.89; per-predictor VIF median 2.09, maximum 4.53), adjustment in high-R-squared cohorts removes part of the disease contrast by construction. The base model is the primary specification; the adjusted model is reported alongside it for every state.
*Single-cell layers.* Four datasets contributed contrasts: colorectal cancer (tumour versus non-tumour tissue; 29 donors), inflammatory bowel disease (disease versus healthy donors; 18 donors), gastric cancer (tumour versus adjacent tissue and versus non-pathological donors; 59 donors) and asthma (allergic asthma versus control; 8 donors). One further dataset (lung adenocarcinoma, 117,266 cells) was reviewed and excluded because all cells come from tumour tissue and no control arm exists. Cell-level module scores were averaged to donor means per cell type and arm; arms required at least 3 donors, a paired signed-rank test was used when at least 5 donors contributed both arms, an unpaired test only when the arms were donor-disjoint, and comparisons satisfying neither condition were reported as not testable. Multiplicity was controlled by Benjamini-Hochberg within families defined by context x module x comparison type. Cells labelled malignant inside non-tumour samples (358 cells) are flagged, not reassigned.
*TCGA-derived layer.* Molecular data were reorganised for six cancer contexts. Because the oesophageal source study contains both adenocarcinoma and squamous carcinoma, the analysed layers were restricted to the squamous subset identified from patient-level histological annotation (96 patients; 94 with survival data); in this version the mutation-prevalence denominator was corrected accordingly, since it previously counted all 185 oesophageal profiles, whereas the expression, copy-number and survival layers were already squamous-restricted. Mutation status is reported only for samples present in the source study's mutation-profiled list, as 'mutation reported' or 'no mutation reported in the selected mutation data'; samples outside that list are labelled not assayed/unknown.
*Source versions and licences.* Source releases, accessions and licence notes are shipped (01_cohort_registry/source_versions.csv), and the single-cell inputs are identified by their CELLxGENE dataset versions and collections (CRC dataset_version_id 81bd8922-b69a-45ea-9b2a-032c480c0e37 in collection 1cbfb478-2c7f-4d15-b522-9f74e9fe52a8; IBD dataset_version_id 5e75dd8f-5c5f-4056-ade2-117748429022 in collection 7c7bd6c2-925b-4034-baab-620ef1b760e1; LUAD dataset_version_id 229e74ee-9b3e-4ab2-bddb-9eccbac85b48 in collection 0bebef1a-4607-4584-9070-dacf89a0d635; STAD dataset_version_id c10345ad-7e42-4a1b-ae78-d4d1547b8d06 in collection f11cb29c-b546-4738-9bd8-66ea621a7bd5), which were re-verified against the CELLxGENE API (cell counts and titles agree with the locally processed files). Derived data are released under CC-BY-4.0 by the authors of this resource; upstream data remain under the terms of the originating repositories (GEO series terms, TCGA/GDC open-access terms, CELLxGENE dataset terms), and no controlled-access data were used. Users redistributing source matrices must observe the upstream terms.
*Validation rules and thresholds.* Reported checks use explicit rules (Supplementary Table S5): common-gene sensitivity uses the module members present in every cohort, flags a module when the effect direction disagrees with full-member scoring in at least 20% of cohorts, and marks it not scorable when fewer than two members are shared, with modules sharing only two members flagged as low-information; proportional hazards is assessed with scaled Schoenfeld residuals in the age-adjusted model and flagged at p<0.05; exact reproduction means an absolute difference of zero in the recomputed statistic, whereas rank agreement is reported as a Spearman correlation and is never described as exact reproduction.

**Technical Validation**
Coverage and composition of the resource are shown in Figure 1; the validation evidence is shown in Figures 2 and 3. Sample identity: no sample identifier is duplicated across the 26 case-control cohorts, and every sample in the annotation resolves to a registry entry. Design: pairing is metadata-derived (15 paired cohorts) and the 9 cohorts without patient identifiers are analysed unpaired and flagged. Histology and mutation layer: the oesophageal layer is restricted to squamous histology (96 patients), where TP53 is reported mutated in 86 patients (89.58%), EGFR in 2 (2.08%), and neither KRAS nor ERBB2 in any patient of the profiled list; EGFR high-level amplification is present in 14 of 96 copy-number-profiled squamous patients (14.58%, with gain or amplification in 64, 66.67%). Exact reproduction: the 49 cohort-module effects and their standard errors of three independently recomputed bulk cohorts agreed with the shipped values to a maximum absolute difference of 0 (3 cohorts; sample-level scores Spearman 1.00); refitting the Cox models from the shipped patient scores reproduced all 68 shipped hazard ratios with a maximum absolute difference of 0 (LUAD, CRC and squamous oesophageal contexts, univariable and age-plus-stage). Rank agreement (not exact reproduction): rebuilding the TCGA patient-level scores from the gene-level table reproduced the shipped scores in rank order (Spearman 0.95-0.98 across 20,451 patient-module pairs), the residual absolute differences arising from the aggregation order, which is now stated explicitly in Methods. Composition: independently recomputed xCell compartment scores agreed with the shipped values (Pearson 0.71-0.95, Spearman 0.48-0.94). Comparability: across the 165 context-module pairs with at least two cohorts, 112 (68%) have effects of the same sign in every contributing cohort, and the number of pooled states surviving BH control changes from 13 with all cohorts to 13 at coverage 0.60, 12 at 0.80 and 6 at 0.90 (k = 165 to 130 pairs), so comparability degrades gradually with coverage rather than being an all-or-nothing property. Survival layer: proportional hazards was not violated for any of the 17 tested module features, which is evidence of absence of a detectable violation rather than proof that the assumption holds. Single-cell: 240 comparisons are shipped (168 tumour-versus-adjacent, 40 case-versus-healthy, 32 cell-identity), each labelled with the test actually used (72 paired by donor, 56 donor-disjoint unpaired, 32 cell-identity contrasts, 80 reported as not testable); 37 comparisons reach FDR below 0.05 within their context x module x comparison-type family and are descriptive rather than validated biology. Module scores are expression-derived and do not measure phosphorylation or pathway activity. Module coverage flags: FGFR_AXIS, JAK_STAT, SRC_FAK show direction disagreement in at least 20% of cohorts and TAM_AXL_AXIS, TIE_ANGPT_AXIS have too few shared members to re-score.

**Usage Notes**
Which layer to use (Supplementary Table S8): GSVA z-scores within a cohort for within-cohort contrasts; TCGA member-mean z-scores for patient-level comparisons across tumour contexts; single-cell member means for cell-type-resolved questions; composition scores only as descriptors or as a sensitivity-analysis covariate. Users should note that (i) scores are comparable within a cohort and across cohorts only as effect sizes, and the shipped consistency and coverage-sensitivity tables quantify how far that holds; (ii) module coverage must be checked before any cross-cohort comparison, and the common-gene flags identify modules that should not be compared across platforms; (iii) the three score layers must not be concatenated; (iv) composition scores are enrichment scores, not proportions, and share genes with the module scores, so adjusted coefficients should be read as sensitivity analyses; (v) the same disease label in different layers does not imply the same patients, and the resource is not a patient-level multi-omics resource; (vi) the survival, driver and single-cell analyses shipped here are exploratory and include negative and not-estimable results; (vii) cohorts without patient identifiers cannot be analysed as paired, and this is flagged rather than assumed. Maintenance: the resource carries a version number, and updates are published as new versions of the deposit so that earlier DOIs remain citable; the changelog records what changed between versions.

The curated resource is deposited in Zenodo under a CC-BY-4.0 licence ([DOI to be inserted after Zenodo deposit]); the code repository is https://github.com/sushuqiong/cross-disease-transcriptomic-resource (renamed from egfr-pathway-context-atlas; the former URL redirects) and is archived in the deposit.
